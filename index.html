Why the demand side comes first Phase 2

This is a matching problem with imperfect information, and demand isn't static. The side with the most flexibility is the student - and because her accommodations persist on her record, the platform knows she needs a DR room before she picks a time. Show her where a room is open, within her exam window. Offer a waitlist. Never block a slot.

The line: this shows a student where her accommodation can actually be met, within her instructor's exam window - never where the center would prefer her. Nothing is reserved until the coordinator places her. What's measured is how many hinted students move to an open slot or join the waitlist, whether that reduces conflicts or just moves them, and how many keep their original time - which is also a valid answer.

Every slot stays bookable. The hint is computed from the room model (Phase 1) and the student's own record - rules, not a model. Events: booking_hint_shown, booking_slot_changed, dr_waitlist_joined.

Two horizons, two products. This view serves the director three weeks out, when student workers and overflow rooms get decided. The day board serves the coordinator in the last three days, when the bookings are real.
